How Maria's life changed in 12 weeks.

By Aisha Kuria · Updated March 14, 2026

Photo by Asher Goldman / RCSI · all rights reserved.

Maria Salinas spent eleven years working two cleaning jobs to send money home to her mother in Oaxaca. The hours were brutal, the wages unstable, and she was tired by 41. "I was tired of being tired," she says now, sitting at the small wooden table in her North Portland apartment. A pot of beans simmers on the stove behind her.

In November 2024, a flyer changed things. Stapled to a bulletin board outside the Hollywood library, it advertised a free 12-week culinary training program — tools provided, lunch included, transit pass paid for. Maria laughed at first. "Things that say free are not free," she remembers thinking. But the program coordinator, Wendy Ortiz, called her three times before Maria agreed to come in for an interview.

"They didn't ask what I had done before. They asked what I wanted next. Nobody asks that."

By week three, Maria was working a hot line at the program's pop-up restaurant, served from a converted shipping container in the parking lot of the old Madison South YMCA. By week eight, she had a knife she could call her own. By week twelve, she had three job offers — two in restaurants, one as a private chef for a family in Beaverton.

She took the family job. The hours are predictable. The pay is $26 an hour, with health insurance and three weeks off. Last summer, she sent her mother enough to fix the roof.
